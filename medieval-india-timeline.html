A. BRIEF SOURCE AUDIT AND OVERALL PERIOD ARCHITECTURE

1. Source Audit

Primary Source — Medieval India by Satish Chandra (NCERT Class XI/XII): Provides the narrative backbone for political developments, dynastic chronologies, administrative structures, land revenue systems, and socio-economic dynamics from the 8th to the 18th century.
Supplementary Source — Themes in Indian History, Part II (NCERT Class XII): Offers thematic depth through primary source extracts, architectural/archaeological case studies, and specialized thematic lenses:
  • Theme 5 (Through the Eyes of Travellers): Focuses on Al-Biruni (11th c.), Ibn Battuta (14th c.), and François Bernier (17th c.).
  • Theme 6 (Bhakti-Sufi Traditions): Explores religious transformations from the 8th to the 18th century (Alvars, Nayanars, Virashaivas, Chishti/Suhrawardi/Naqshbandi silsilas, Kabir, Nanak, Mirabai).
  • Theme 7 (An Imperial Capital: Vijayanagara): Detailed architectural and socio-political analysis of Vijayanagara (14th–16th c.).
  • Theme 8 (Peasants, Zamindars and the State): Agrarian structure, village communities, revenue administration, and silver monetization in the Mughal era (16th–17th c.).
  • Theme 9 (Kings and Chronicles): Ideology, court culture, manuscript production, and imperial administration of the Mughals.

2. Overall Period Architecture (c. 750 CE – mid-18th Century)

PERIOD 1: MASTER CHRONOLOGY (c. 750 CE – 1206 CE)

711 CE
Event: Arab conquest of Sind led by Muhammad bin Qasim.
Explanation: Sind was conquered by the Umayyad Caliphate's general, incorporating the region into the Caliph’s domain.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Established the earliest Islamic political presence in North-Western India. It opened coastal and overland trade channels between West Asia and the Indian subcontinent, introducing Arab merchants to Western Indian ports.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Early Islamic Inroads & Political Foundations.
  • Subtopic: Arab Conquest of Sind and its long-term socio-economic impact.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: Study NCERT Theme 6, Section 5.1 ("Faiths of rulers and subjects").
UPSC Relevance: BOTH | Syllabus Theme: Salient features of Medieval Indian History / Foreign Contacts.
Citations:
c. 500 – 800 CE(Socio-Religious Layer)
Event: Early Tamil Bhakti Movement led by Nayanars (Shaiva) and Alvars (Vaishnava).
Explanation: Poet-saints such as Appar, Sambandar, and Sundaramurti (Nayanars) and Karaikkal Ammaiyar (female Shaiva saint) composed devotional poetry in Tamil.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Initiated a popular devotional protest against orthodox Brahmanical ritualism and the dominance of Jainism/Buddhism in South India. It accommodated lower castes and women into devotional spaces and laid the foundation for royal temple patronage by Pallavas, Pandyas, and Cholas.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Bhakti Movement in South India.
  • Subtopic: Social composition, gender dimensions (Karaikkal Ammaiyar), and critique of caste.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: Read NCERT Theme 6, Section 2 ("Poems of Prayer: Early Traditions of Bhakti").
UPSC Relevance: GS-I MAINS | Syllabus Theme: Indian Culture – Literature, Philosophy & Social Reform Movements.
Citations:
c. 750 CE
Event: Foundation of the Pala Dynasty in Bengal and Bihar by Gopala.
Explanation: Gopala was elected by regional chieftains to end the period of political anarchy (matsyanyaya) following the collapse of Harsha’s empire and the Guptas.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Stabilized eastern India and established Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism as state-patronized religions. Created the political base for Bengal’s entry into the Tripartite Struggle.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Regional Kingdoms of North/East India.
  • Subtopic: Origin of the Palas, election of Gopala, and Buddhist monastic patronage.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: Satish Chandra, Chapter 1 (Northern India: Age of the Three Empires).
UPSC Relevance: PRELIMS | Syllabus Theme: Political History of Early Medieval India.
c. 753 CE
Event: Rise of the Rashtrakuta Dynasty in the Deccan under Dantidurga.
Explanation: Dantidurga overthrew the Chalukyas of Badami, performed the Hiranyagarbha ritual at Ujjain, and established Manyakheta (Malkhed) as the imperial capital.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Shifted the geopolitical center of the Deccan. The Rashtrakutas served as a bridge between Northern and Southern India, dominating the Western coast and controlling trans-peninsular trade routes.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Deccan Kingdoms.
  • Subtopic: Rashtrakuta state formation, rock-cut architecture (Ellora Kailashnath), and patronage to Kannada/Sanskrit literature.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: Satish Chandra, Chapter 1.
UPSC Relevance: BOTH | Syllabus Theme: Art & Architecture / Regional Dynasties.
c. 780 – 810 CE
Event: Reign of Dharmapala (Pala Empire) & Commencement of the Tripartite Struggle.
Explanation: Dharmapala expanded Pala power into the Indo-Gangetic plain, captured Kanauj, and held a grand imperial assembly (durbar). He founded the Vikramashila Monastery and revived Nalanda.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Triggered the Tripartite Struggle between the Palas, Gurjara-Pratiharas (Vatsaraja/Nagabhata II), and Rashtrakutas (Dhruva/Govinda III) for control of Kanauj and the fertile doab. This centuries-long conflict exhausted all three imperial lines, leading to political fragmentation into localized Rajput clan-states.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Tripartite Struggle for Kanauj.
  • Subtopic: Causes, major battles, involvement of Palas, Pratiharas, and Rashtrakutas, and ultimate political exhaustion.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: Satish Chandra, Chapter 1.
UPSC Relevance: BOTH | Syllabus Theme: Political Geography & Imperial Struggles.
c. 814 – 878 CE
Event: Reign of Amoghavarsha I (Rashtrakuta Dynasty).
Explanation: Long and peaceful reign marked by patronage of Jainism, literature, and art. Amoghavarsha authored Kavirajamarga (the earliest available work on poetics in Kannada) and Prashnottara Ratnamalika in Sanskrit.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Represented the cultural zenith of the Rashtrakutas. Arab merchant Sulaiman described his empire as one of the four great empires of the world (alongside Rome, China, and the Caliphate).
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Cultural Contributions of Deccan Dynasties.
  • Subtopic: Amoghavarsha I, Jain patronage, Kannada literary development.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: Satish Chandra, Chapter 1.
UPSC Relevance: PRELIMS | Syllabus Theme: Literature and Cultural Patrons.
c. 836 – 885 CE
Event: Reign of Mihir Bhoja (Gurjara-Pratihara Dynasty).
Explanation: Re-established Pratihara supremacy over Western and Central India, recovering Kanauj and making it the capital. Adopted the title Adivaraha (inscriptions and silver coins).
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Created a powerful buffer state in North-Western India that held back Arab expansion from Sind for over a century.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Gurjara-Pratihara Empire.
  • Subtopic: Mihir Bhoja, resistance to Arab raids, land grants, and temple architecture.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: Satish Chandra, Chapter 1.
UPSC Relevance: PRELIMS | Syllabus Theme: Early Medieval Political Units.
c. 850 CE
Event: Capture of Thanjavur by Vijayalaya; Foundation of the Imperial Chola Dynasty.
Explanation: Vijayalaya, a feudatory of the Pallavas, captured Thanjavur from the Muttaraiyar chieftains and established a new Chola line.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Initiated the transition of the Cholas from a local chiefdom into an imperial maritime state in South India.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Imperial Cholas of Tanjore.
  • Subtopic: Rise of Vijayalaya, territorial consolidation in Tamilakam.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: NCERT Theme 7, p. 172; Satish Chandra, Chapter 2.
UPSC Relevance: PRELIMS | Syllabus Theme: State Formation in Peninsular India.
Citations:
c. 907 – 955 CE
Event: Reign of Parantaka I (Chola Empire) & Royal Institutionalization of Tamil Hymns.
Explanation: Parantaka I expanded Chola power against the Pandyas and Sri Lanka. Inscriptional evidence from c. 945 CE reveals that he consecrated metal images of Shaiva saints (Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar) in a Shiva temple to be carried in processions.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Demonstrated the strategy of Chola monarchs using popular Bhakti traditions to legitimise royal authority, build agrarian alliances with Vellala peasants, and integrate local cults into state-sponsored temples.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Chola State and Religion.
  • Subtopic: Inscriptional evidence of temple consecration, Uttiramerur inscriptions on village self-governance (Sabha/Ur).
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: NCERT Theme 6, p. 146.
UPSC Relevance: BOTH | Syllabus Theme: Local Self-Government & Temple Polity.
Citations:
c. 973 – 1048 CE(Biographical Window)
Event: Lifespan and Work of Muhammad ibn Ahmad Abu Raihan al-Biruni.
Explanation: Born in Khwarizm (973 CE), educated in Syriac, Arabic, Persian, Hebrew, and Sanskrit. Taken to Ghazni as a hostage by Sultan Mahmud in 1017 CE. Accompanied Ghaznavid armies to the Punjab and northern India.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Authored the Kitab-ul-Hind (in Arabic, 80 chapters). Developed a geometric method of comparative cultural analysis. Identified three major barriers to understanding Indian society: Language (Sanskrit), Religious differences, and Insularity/self-absorption of the Brahmanas.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Foreign Travellers’ Accounts.
  • Subtopic: Al-Biruni’s Kitab-ul-Hind, critique of the caste system (comparison with Persia), and observation of Sanskritic traditions.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: NCERT Theme 5, Sections 1 & 4.
UPSC Relevance: BOTH | Syllabus Theme: Sources of Medieval History / Travellers’ Accounts.
Citations:
c. 985 – 1014 CE
Event: Reign of Rajaraja I (Imperial Chola Empire).
Explanation: Rajaraja I conducted extensive military campaigns, destroying the Chera navy at Trivandrum, conquering northern Sri Lanka (Anuradhapura), and annexing the Maldives. Built the Brihadishvara Temple at Thanjavur (completed c. 1010 CE). Initiated the formal compilation and organization of Tamil Shaiva hymns into the Tevaram text.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Transformed the Chola state into a thalassocratic (maritime) empire. Used massive stone temples (raya gopurams, detailed inscriptions) to proclaim divine support and institutionalize land revenue measurement (Kaniikatan).
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Imperial Cholas.
  • Subtopic: Naval expansion, administrative organization, revenue surveys, Brihadishvara Temple architecture.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: NCERT Theme 6, p. 146; Theme 7, p. 172.
UPSC Relevance: BOTH | Syllabus Theme: Architecture & Naval History.
Citations:
c. 1000 – 1027 CE
Event: Invasions of Sultan Mahmud of Ghazni into Northern India.
Explanation: Mahmud of Ghazni conducted 17 plunder raids into North India, targeting urban temple centers including Nagarkot, Thanesar, Mathura, Kanauj, and Somnath (1025–26 CE).
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Smashed the military coalition of the Hindu Shahis (Jayapala/Anandapala) and annexed the Punjab to the Ghaznavid Empire. Plundered immense wealth to embellish Ghazni, while weakening the economic and military foundation of Rajput states in the Doab. Punjab’s inclusion in the Ghaznavid empire created sustained cross-cultural contacts, facilitating Al-Biruni’s studies.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Invasions from the North-West.
  • Subtopic: Nature and motives of Mahmud of Ghazni’s raids, annexation of Punjab, and political consequences.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: Satish Chandra, Chapter 5; NCERT Theme 5, p. 117.
UPSC Relevance: BOTH | Syllabus Theme: Military History & Foreign Incursions.
Citations:
c. 1014 – 1044 CE
Event: Reign of Rajendra I (Imperial Chola Empire).
Explanation: Rajendra I carried Chola naval power to its apex. Completed the conquest of Sri Lanka (capturing King Mahinda V), led a military expedition to the river Ganga (Gangaikonda), defeating Mahipala I of Bengal, and launched a major naval campaign against the Srivijaya Kingdom in Southeast Asia (Sumatra/Malaya) in 1025 CE. Founded the new capital Gangaikondacholapuram.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Protected Indian maritime trade routes to China and Southeast Asia from Malay piracy. Secured Chola dominance over the Bay of Bengal (often termed a "Chola Lake") and promoted long-distance merchant guilds (Manigramam, Ayyavole-500).
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Chola Overseas Expansion.
  • Subtopic: Srivijaya naval expedition, Gangaikondacholapuram, trade guilds, and maritime trade.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: Satish Chandra, Chapter 2; NCERT Theme 6, p. 146.
UPSC Relevance: BOTH | Syllabus Theme: India’s External Trade & Maritime History.
Citations:
1039 – 1073 CE
Event: Arrival and Death of Abu’l Hasan al-Hujwiri (Data Ganj Bakhsh) at Lahore.
Explanation: Hujwiri was brought to Lahore as a captive of the invading Ghaznavid army in 1039 CE. Settled in Lahore and authored the Persian treatise Kashf-ul-Mahjub ("Unveiling of the Veiled") to explain tasawwuf (Sufism). Died in 1073 CE; his tomb became the Data Darbar shrine.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Represents the earliest institutional foundation of Sufi literary and devotional traditions in the Indian subcontinent.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Growth of Sufism in India.
  • Subtopic: Hujwiri, Kashf-ul-Mahjub, and early Sufi literature in Punjab.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: NCERT Theme 6, p. 155 & p. 166.
UPSC Relevance: PRELIMS | Syllabus Theme: Sufi Movements & Religious Texts.
Citations:
c. 1106 – 1168 CE
Event: Emergence of the Virashaiva / Lingayat Movement in Karnataka led by Basavanna.
Explanation: Basavanna, a Brahmana minister at the court of the Kalachuri ruler, initiated a socio-religious movement in Karnataka. His followers, known as Virashaivas (heroes of Shiva) or Lingayats (wearers of the linga), expressed their devotional philosophy through short Kannada prose-poems called Vachanas.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Radically challenged the orthodox Brahmanical framework, rejecting the caste system, ritual pollution, Vedic authority, sacrifice, child marriage, and the subordination of women. Practiced burial of the dead instead of cremation (united with Shiva upon death).
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Virashaiva/Lingayat Tradition.
  • Subtopic: Basavanna, Vachanas, social radicalism, rejection of caste and Dharmashastric funeral rites.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: NCERT Theme 6, Section 3 ("The Virashaiva Tradition in Karnataka").
UPSC Relevance: BOTH | Syllabus Theme: Social Reform Movements in Medieval India.
Citations:
1175 – 1192 CE
Event: Campaigns of Mu'izzuddin Muhammad Ghori & The Battles of Tarain.
Explanation: Muhammad Ghori launched invasions to establish a territorial empire in India.
  • 1178 CE: Defeated at Kayadara (near Mt. Abu) by the Gujarat Chalukya forces under Queen Naiki Devi / Mularaja II.
  • 1191 CE (First Battle of Tarain): Prithviraj Chauhan III (Chahamana ruler of Delhi-Ajmer) defeated Muhammad Ghori.
  • 1192 CE (Second Battle of Tarain): Muhammad Ghori defeated and captured Prithviraj Chauhan III.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: The Second Battle of Tarain (1192 CE) was a major turning point in Indian history. It broke the military backbone of Rajput resistance in North India, paved the way for the establishment of Turkish rule in the Delhi-Ajmer belt, and led to the collapse of the traditional Brahmana-Rajput socio-political monopoly.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Ghorid Conquests and Rajput Resistance.
  • Subtopic: Causes for Rajput defeat, strategic significance of Tarain, transition from plunder raids to territorial conquest.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: Satish Chandra, Chapter 6; NCERT Theme 6, p. 148; Theme 7, p. 193.
UPSC Relevance: BOTH | Syllabus Theme: Decisive Battles in Indian History / Establishment of Turkish Rule.
Citations:
1194 CE
Event: Battle of Chandawar.
Explanation: Muhammad Ghori defeated Jaichandra, the Gahadavala ruler of Kanauj, near the Yamuna river.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Destroyed Gahadavala power and extended Ghorid control over the central Indo-Gangetic plain up to Banaras, opening Bihar and Bengal to Turkish military expansion.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Ghorid Expansion.
  • Subtopic: Fall of the Gahadavalas, conquest of the Gangetic valley.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: Satish Chandra, Chapter 6.
UPSC Relevance: PRELIMS | Syllabus Theme: Territorial Shifts in Early Medieval India.
c. 1199 – 1206 CE
Event: Eastern Campaigns of Ikhtiyaruddin Muhammad bin Bakhtiyar Khalji.
Explanation: Bakhtiyar Khalji, a general of Muhammad Ghori, led rapid cavalry raids into Bihar and Bengal. Destroyed the monastic university centres of Nalanda and Vikramashila (c. 1202–1203 CE) and expelled Lakshmana Sena from Nadia, establishing his base at Lakhnauti (Northern Bengal).
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Ended the Sena dynasty's hegemony, erased organized Buddhist monastic institutions in eastern India, and established the easternmost frontier outpost of Islamic rule in Bengal.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Turkish Expansion into Eastern India.
  • Subtopic: Destruction of Buddhist universities, fall of Nadia, establishment of Lakhnauti principality.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: Satish Chandra, Chapter 6.
UPSC Relevance: BOTH | Syllabus Theme: Impact on Educational Centers & Cultural Institutions.
1206 CE
Event: Assassination of Muhammad Ghori & Accession of Qutbuddin Aibak; Establishment of the Delhi Sultanate.
Explanation: Following the assassination of Muhammad Ghori at Dhamyak (1206 CE), his Turkish slave-general Qutbuddin Aibak severed ties with Ghazni and assumed independent sovereign authority at Lahore/Delhi, founding the Mamluk (Slave) Dynasty.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Formally marks the beginning of the Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526 CE). Unified Northern India under a centralized military monarchy, replaced the regional feudal structure, and integrated India into the wider Islamic diplomatic and commercial networks.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Foundation of the Delhi Sultanate.
  • Subtopic: Qutbuddin Aibak, Slave Dynasty, administrative transition from Ghorid military governorship to independent Sultanate.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: NCERT Theme 6, p. 148–149; Theme 7, p. 193; Satish Chandra, Chapter 6.
UPSC Relevance: BOTH | Syllabus Theme: Foundation of Sultanate Architecture & State System.

Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Formally severed Indian political destinies from Central Asian Ghorid politics. Established an independent, centralized Islamic state structure in North India. Initiated the construction of the Qutb Minar and Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque at Delhi.

1540 – 1545 CE
Event: Reign of Sher Shah Suri & Administrative, Fiscal, and Infrastructure Reforms.
Explanation: Sher Shah consolidated an empire stretching from Bengal to the Indus. He implemented far-reaching structural reforms:
  • Agrarian Administration: Implemented a revenue assessment system based on direct land measurement (paimaish). Formulated a central schedule of crop output rates (Ray), dividing land into good, middling, and bad, with the state demand fixed at one-third of average produce, payable in cash or kind. Title deeds (Patta) and deeds of acceptance (Qabuliyat) were exchanged with peasants, and a 2.5-seer/bigha emergency grain tax was levied for famine reserves.
  • Currency & Weights: Standardized currency by issuing a pure silver coin (Rupiya, weighing 178 grains) and copper Dam, replacing debased mixed-metal coinage. Standardized weights and measures empire-wide.
  • Infrastructure & Communication: Restored the Grand Trunk Road (Sadak-i-Azam) from Sonargaon (Bengal) to the Indus. Built 1,700 Sarais (fortified inns with separate lodgings and food for Hindus and Muslims, managed by Brahmanas/custodians), which functioned as market-towns (qasbas) and postal relays (Dak-Chowki).
  • Provincial & Local Governance: Structured local administration into Sarkars (under Shiqdar-i-Shiqdran and Munsif-i-Munsifan) and Parganas (under Shiqdar, Munsif/Amil, and clerks in Persian/Hindavi). Enforced local responsibility for law and order, holding village headmen (Muqaddams) and zamindars liable for highway robberies and murders.
  • Military Organization: Restored Alauddin Khalji’s military practices—direct soldier recruitment, descriptive rolls (Chehra), and horse branding (Dagh).
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Laid the administrative and fiscal framework later inherited, refined, and expanded by Akbar. The Rupiya remained India's standard monetary unit for centuries.

Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Abolishing Jizya laid the institutional foundation for a non-sectarian state (Sulh-i Kul). 1580 CE
Event: Promulgation of the Dahsala System, Introduction of 12 Subas & Arrival of 1st Jesuit Mission.
Explanation:
  • Dahsala System: Formulated by Finance Minister Raja Todar Mal, this revenue framework calculated the 10-year average yield and prevailing market prices (1570–1580 CE) for various crops. The state demand was fixed in cash at one-third of average produce per bigha, utilizing bamboo measuring rods linked with iron rings (tanab). Lands were classified into Polaj (annually cultivated), Parauti (fallow 1–2 years), Chachar (fallow 3–4 years), and Banjar (fallow 5+ years).
  • Provincial Restructuring: Divided the empire into 12 Subas: Bengal, Bihar, Allahabad, Awadh, Agra, Delhi, Lahore, Multan, Kabul, Ajmer, Malwa, and Gujarat. Replicated central administrative roles (Subadar, Diwan, Bakhshi, Sadr, Qazi, Waqia-Navis) at the provincial level.
  • Jesuit Mission: The First Jesuit Mission, led by Padre Rudolf Acquaviva and Father Antonio Monserrate, arrived at Fatehpur Sikri from Goa.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Provided a stable fiscal basis for land revenue assessment across North India. Standardized provincial governance (Suba-Sarkar-Pargana) under a system of checks and balances.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Todar Mal’s Dahsala, Land Classification & Provincial Administration.

1582 CE
Event: Promulgation of Tauhid-i-Ilahi (Din-i-Ilahi) & Closure of Ibadat Khana.
Explanation: Akbar discontinued the bitter debates of the Ibadat Khana and introduced Tauhid-i-Ilahi (Divine Monotheism)—an eclectic order of discipleship. Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Functioned not as a new formal religion, but as an elite order designed to foster loyalty among court elites. It embodied the state philosophy of Sulh-i Kul (absolute peace and universal harmony).

B. DETAILED YEAR-BY-YEAR MASTER TIMELINE FOR PERIOD 6 (1707 CE – 1761 CE)

PERIOD 6: MASTER CHRONOLOGY (1707 CE – 1761 CE)

1707 CE
Event: Death of Emperor Aurangzeb, Release of Shahu & War of Succession.
Explanation: Aurangzeb died at Ahmednagar in March 1707. Prince Muhammad Azam Shah, marching north to claim the throne, released Shivaji’s grandson Shahu from imperial captivity to create a rift among the Marathas. Shahu returned to Maharashtra, defeated Maharani Tarabai’s forces at the Battle of Khed (1707), and was crowned at Satara. Meanwhile, Prince Muazzam defeated his brothers Azam Shah (Battle of Jajau) and Kam Bakhsh, ascending the Mughal throne as Bahadur Shah I (Shah Alam I).
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Initiated the political division of the Marathas into two seats (Satara and Kolhapur) and marked the beginning of the political decline of the central Mughal monarchy.
Study Pointer:
  • Topic: Post-Aurangzeb Succession & Maratha Civil War.
  • Subtopic: Release of Shahu, Battle of Khed, accession of Bahadur Shah I.
  • NCERT / SC Guidance: Satish Chandra, Chapter 18; NCERT Theme 8 Timeline.
UPSC Relevance: BOTH | Syllabus Theme: Dynastic Transitions & Regional Re-alignments.

Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Transformed Sikh political resistance in Punjab into a agrarian peasant uprising against Mughal zamindars and faujdars (Wazir Khan of Sirhind).

1709 – 1712 CE
Event: Conciliation Policy of Bahadur Shah I (Shah-i-Bekhabar).
Explanation: Bahadur Shah I reversed Aurangzeb’s rigid policies: restored Amber and Marwar to Ajit Singh and Jai Singh II, recognized Shahu's claim to Sardeshmukhi (10%) of the Deccan (while withholding Chauth), and granted high mansabs to Rajput princes.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Temporarily stabilized relations with Rajputs and Marathas, but reckless grants of land and titles drained the imperial treasury (Khalisa lands), earning him the title Shah-i-Bekhabar from historian Khafi Khan.

1712 – 1713 CE
Event: Reign of Jahandar Shah, Ascendancy of Zulfiqar Khan & Abolition of Jizya.
Explanation: Following Bahadur Shah’s death, Jahandar Shah won the throne with the support of Zulfiqar Khan, who assumed the office of Wazir. Zulfiqar Khan formally abolished Jizya, granted both Chauth and Sardeshmukhi of the Deccan to Shahu, and sought alliances with Rajput rajas. However, he introduced the system of revenue-farming (Ijara).
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Ijara led to the reckless financial exploitation of the peasantry by revenue contractors, ruining agricultural infrastructure and accelerating agrarian distress across North India.

1713 – 1719 CE
Event: Reign of Farrukhsiyar, Rise of Sayyid Brothers, Appointment of Peshwa Balaji Vishwanath (1713) & EIC Farman (1717).
Explanation:
  • Rise of Sayyid Brothers: Farrukhsiyar overthrew Jahandar Shah with the help of the Sayyid Brothers—Abdullah Khan (Wazir) and Husain Ali Khan (Mir Bakhshi)—known as the "Kingmakers".
  • Appointment of Balaji Vishwanath (1713 CE): Chhatrapati Shahu appointed Balaji Vishwanath as Peshwa (Prime Minister), establishing the hereditary Peshwaship and initiating the administrative consolidation of the Maratha state.
  • Execution of Banda Bahadur (1716 CE): Imperial forces captured Banda Singh Bahadur at Gurdas Nangal; Banda and hundreds of Sikh followers were executed in Delhi.
  • English EIC Farman (1717 CE): Farrukhsiyar issued a royal farman to the John Surman mission, granting the British East India Company duty-free trade privileges in Bengal for an annual payment of Rs. 3,000 and the right to issue Dastaks (trade passes).
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: The 1717 Farman became the commercial foundation for British expansion in Bengal. Balaji Vishwanath reorganized Maratha finances through the Saranjami system, distributing revenue assignments to Maratha sardars (Gaekwad, Holkar, Scindia, Pawar).

1719 CE
Event: The Maratha-Sayyid Treaty (Farman of 1719) & Overthrow of Farrukhsiyar.
Explanation: Peshwa Balaji Vishwanath negotiated a treaty with Sayyid Husain Ali Khan:
  • The Mughal Emperor recognized Shahu’s sovereign title to Shivaji's Swarajya.
  • Granted the Marathas the right to collect Chauth (25%) and Sardeshmukhi (10%) across the six Mughal subas of the Deccan (Khandesh, Berar, Aurangabad, Bidar, Hyderabad, Bijapur).
  • In return, Shahu agreed to maintain a contingent of 15,000 Maratha cavalry for imperial service and preserve peace in the Deccan.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: A Maratha army marched to Delhi alongside Husain Ali Khan—the first time Maratha troops entered the imperial capital. Farrukhsiyar was deposed, blinded, and killed, demonstrating the subordination of the Mughal court to regional military backing.

1739 CE
Event: Invasion of Nadir Shah of Iran, Battle of Karnal & Sacking of Delhi.
Explanation: Nadir Shah, the Afsharid ruler of Persia, invaded India via Kabul and Ghazni. He met Muhammad Shah’s imperial army at the Battle of Karnal (February 1739) and routed the Mughals within three hours. Nadir Shah entered Delhi alongside Muhammad Shah. Following a local riot in which Persian soldiers were killed, Nadir Shah ordered a general massacre (qatl-i-am) of 30,000 citizens. Persian forces plundered Delhi for 57 days, seizing treasure worth 70 crore rupees, including the Peacock Throne (Takht-i-Taus), the Koh-i-Noor and Darya-i-Noor diamonds, and annexing all Mughal territories west of the Indus River (including Kabul and Sindh).
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Exposed the complete military weakness of the Mughal Empire to foreign powers and regional Indian rulers. Stripped the imperial treasury, destroyed Delhi's commercial prosperity, and paved the way for subsequent Afghan invasions by Ahmad Shah Abdali.

1761 CE(January 14)
Event: Third Battle of Panipat & Collapse of the Maratha Drive for All-India Hegemony.
Explanation: The Maratha imperial army commanded by Sadashivrao Bhau and Nominal Commander Prince Vishwasrao clashed with Ahmad Shah Abdali, backed by Najib-ud-Daula (Rohilla Afghan chief) and Shuja-ud-Daula (Nawab of Awadh), at Panipat.
  • Tactics & Weaknesses: The Marathas were burdened by thousands of non-combatant pilgrims, suffered starvation during a two-month blockade, and lacked support from Northern Hindu powers (Rajputs, Jats under Suraj Mal) due to earlier aggressive Chauth collections.
  • Outcome: Despite heavy fire from Ibrahim Khan Gardi’s artillery, Abdali’s disciplined reserves (zamburaks / swivel guns on camels) turned the tide. Vishwasrao and Sadashivrao Bhau were killed; 28,000 Maratha soldiers perished on the field.
Historical Significance & Cause-and-Effect: Terminated the Maratha bid for an all-India empire (Hindu Pad Padshahi). Severely weakened both Marathas and Mughals, leaving a political vacuum that enabled the British East India Company to expand its control over India. Peshwa Balaji Baji Rao died of heartbreak in June 1761.
